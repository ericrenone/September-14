## Part I: The Bears, On the Record

**Scott Galloway** — the loudest, most specific, and most repeated. His verdict, reiterated within the last week with the stock near $148: SpaceX is worth **"$10 to $30 a share"** — up to 93% downside — resting on swelling losses and a debt-heavy AI bet, including roughly $25 billion borrowed against the inflated valuation two weeks after the IPO. On Morgan Stanley's $300 target: the stuff of **"romantic comedies."** His structural point: Wall Street has no comparable for this valuation — no precedent, no anchor, no map. The record requires a note in both directions: on IPO day he predicted the stock would "pop out of the gates" (it did, +19% to $161), and he has been calling it crazy overvalued since July near $115 — through a run to $202. The thesis has been durable; the timing has been brutal to anyone who traded it.

**Peter Beck (Rocket Lab CEO)** — the most credentialed skeptic alive, the only other chief executive who regularly flies commercial orbital rockets, and a competitor whose incentive runs *toward* sector enthusiasm. In June he called space valuations **"completely untethered to reality."** This month, offered the chance to defend the sector's prices, he declined entirely — conceding the whole complex could go pop. When the person whose business benefits from the mania won't defend the multiple, the multiple has believers rather than defenders, and believers reprice in gaps.

**Jordi Visser** — the freshest cut, filed this week, and the widest: he places SpaceX in the same bucket as Bitcoin, Anthropic, and OpenAI, with the unifying property stated in five words — **"None of them make money."** The remark matters less for its analysis than its genre: the mainstream financial commentariat now groups the largest IPO in history with speculative assets as a matter of casual taxonomy.

**The bears' shared premise:** the price is a narrative artifact, and narratives have no bid when they break. Galloway supplies the destination, Beck the sector's own confession, Visser the category reassignment. None of the three supplies a date — which is precisely what separates their testimony from the calendar's.

## Part II: The Bulls, On the Record

**Morgan Stanley** — the conviction bull, and the freshest datapoint on the board: it **reiterated its Overweight rating Monday morning**, into the gap-down open, and the stock rallied more than 2% off its lows on the note. The house target of $300 is the one Galloway ridicules; the house itself was co-lead underwriter on the IPO. Both facts are true simultaneously, and both belong in the record.

**The target ladder above:** Macquarie at **$250**; Pivotal at **$220**; a 34-analyst Street skewing heavily to Buy, with the full range spanning roughly $62 to $450 — and almost nothing published between $100 and $140, the exact territory a falling tape would have to cross.

**Bret Johnsen (CFO)** — the company's own testimony, delivered twice in the past week. On the business: SpaceX closed another compute agreement worth **$1.11 billion a month** (roughly $13 billion annualized), and he has **"even more conviction"** in the **$100 billion ARR target** by year-end. On the moat: China has an **"amazing space program"** — but it is **"not on a Starship level."** He has also said Flight 14 itself will be revenue-generating. The CFO's stance is maximal: the fundamentals are compounding faster than the fear.

**Cathie Wood (ARK)** — invested since 2023, pre-IPO: SpaceX is **"at the forefront of one of the most significant technological transformations in history, building critical infrastructure for the future of connectivity, intelligence, and economic activity,"** with ARK explicitly willing to **"look beyond benchmarks [and] challenge consensus views."** Noted for completeness: ARK has also been buying Rocket Lab down 57% from its high — maximalist growth capital finding better risk-reward in the broken escort than the intact flagship.

**Frank Cappelleri (technician, via CNBC, Sept 9):** a **bullish pattern** was emerging in the shares — the chart community's contribution, printed five days and one geopolitical weekend ago.

**The bulls' shared premise:** contracted revenue is real, the moat is real, and the multiple is a claim on a transformation that is actually occurring. Their testimony is strongest about 2028 and silent about Thursday.

## Part III: The Abstainer — The Most Informative Witness of All

**Michael Burry** examined this exact trade and refused it, in public, with numbers. In mid-June, with the stock near $212 and the market cap at $2.8 trillion: **"I am not involved with SpaceX now. Neither short nor, ahem, long."** He priced the bear side out loud — a December 2028 $100 put at ~$25, June 2027 at ~$13, December 2026 at ~$6.75: **"Tempted by that one. But no thank you,"** adding, **"With any luck SPCX will settle up here in the mid-$200s, and vol will drain out of put option chain."** His valuation view needed no position to be brutal: a market cap backed by **less than $20 billion of revenue**, enough to buy the fortunes of Bezos, Zuckerberg, Buffett, and Huang with a trillion left over; **"fundamentally a small space company"**; Berkshire Hathaway — "painstakingly assembled over two century-old lives" — **"eclipsed 2 1/2 times over in just three days."**

Two facts complete his testimony. First, when he did want AI-complex short exposure, he expressed it through **Palantir puts**, bought in late 2025 and still held — the best-known bear alive chose a different ticker and a longer runway. Second, the vol drain he said he was waiting for *arrived*: the VIX crushed 11% to 15.84 last Friday, into the most loaded fortnight of the stock's life. The condition he named has been met. His absence is now a standing question rather than a settled answer.

**Matthew Unterman (S3 Partners)** supplies the positioning data beneath Burry's abstention: short interest around 40 million shares, 5–7% of the tradable float — **"far from a supply-constrained short... The current setup looks more like normal price discovery than a classic short-squeeze candidate."** Translation: the bear side is not crowded, squeeze fuel is limited — and a decline would find little forced covering to bid into it.

## Part IV: The Officials — The Testimony That Moves Wednesday

**Kevin Warsh, Federal Reserve Chair**, wrote the week's script at Jackson Hole in August: the summer's better inflation readings **"do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved... We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do."** That single passage took hike odds from ~30% to a coin flip in a session. The August core CPI at a hotter-than-expected 0.3%, then the pipeline attack, finished the job: markets now price the hike near 86–90%. Schwab's Monday desk commentary states the trap plainly: **"A Fed rate hike may be needed to prevent long-end yields from rising significantly higher, as credibility may be on the line... Failure to hike rates now... would likely be judged harshly by market participants, who could punish with even higher yields... The timing also backs Warsh into a corner."** A 10-year through 5% is the bond market pre-grading Wednesday's exam. One wrinkle Warsh himself added: he has spent months insisting he will not provide forward guidance — meaning the December signal, if it comes, arrives through the projections and the framing rather than a promise.

**Badr Albusaidi, Oman's Foreign Minister**, Sunday night, cancelling the week's one scheduled off-ramp: **"In the interests of consensus the regional meeting set for tomorrow in Salalah has been postponed,"** while remaining **"committed to fostering dialogue that supports stability and lasting cooperation in our region."** The meeting to reopen the Strait of Hormuz was postponed after the pipeline attack — the diplomacy cancelled by the escalation it existed to stop. Bahrain had already refused to attend. A tanker was struck inside the strait the same day.

**Amin Nasser, Saudi Aramco CEO**, on what was lost when the East-West Pipeline shut: the line — 7 million barrels a day of capacity — had done **more to stabilize oil markets than the US-led strategic reserve release.** Riyadh has disclosed neither the damage nor a timeline. Undisclosed timelines are themselves information; fast good news gets announced.

**Sen. Ruben Gallego**, Sunday, on the AI front that opened the market's second flank: **"Dr. Frankenstein is telling us the monster is escaping; help us stop this,"** adding that Washington's current response **"is not meeting the moment."**

## Part V: The Insiders — Words Against Actions

The most consequential testimony of the weekend came from inside the complex, and it splits cleanly into what was said and what was done.

**Said:** the leaders of Anthropic, OpenAI — **and SpaceX's own Elon Musk** — called for a slowdown in frontier AI development on safety grounds. Sam Altman went further on his own listing: an OpenAI IPO this year would be **"ill-advised"** — four weeks after his CFO guided to 2027 at the latest. Dario Amodei's essay argued the labs must decelerate, with the **"toughest dilemma"** being whether China follows. Read the SpaceX-specific sentence inside that news: *the chief executive of a company valued at ~65x sales on an AI-infrastructure story has personally called for slowing the technology the story monetizes.* Whatever its civic merit — and it may be considerable — it is, financially, the founder adding an asterisk to his own multiple.

**Done:** Larry Ellison adopted a plan to sell up to **$7.5 billion** of Oracle — the founder of the AI buildout's most leveraged bellwether ($125B of debt) scheduling distribution days after a blowout AI-demand quarter. Executive sales have simultaneously pressured Rocket Lab. Inside $SPCX, the December 8 wall concentrates every holder's mind: Musk and insiders are locked 366 days, but the staggered 180-day tranches — September 24, October 9, October 24, then the wall — belong to holders for whom every rally is an exit window. The Ellison precedent established that scheduled, lawyered, size distribution is now socially normal inside the complex. The first $SPCX Form 4 of size will not read as an anomaly.
